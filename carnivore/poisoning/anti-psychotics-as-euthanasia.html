Anti-Psychotics and Euthanasia: an ingredient-overlap theory

Note: This page is a speculative theory written by a student. It is not established science and not medical advice. Nothing here has been tested or peer reviewed, and it should not be used to diagnose or treat anything. If you are unwell, please see a clinician.

The idea

I looked at the ingredient lists of some antipsychotic medicines and of some drugs used for euthanasia, and noticed that 39 of the ingredients on each list appear on both. I wondered whether this could mean something about how the medicines work. I now think it most likely does not, as explained below. I am keeping the comparison here as an example of a question and of how to check it.

Why the match probably means little

The shared items are mostly inactive ingredients (excipients): fillers, binders, coatings and preservatives such as corn starch, lactose, magnesium stearate, titanium dioxide, propylene glycol and sodium benzoate. Almost all tablets and injections use the same short list. Matching on them says nothing about the active drug. The active ingredients in the two lists (for example haloperidol, risperidone and olanzapine versus the barbiturates pentobarbital and thiopental) do not overlap.

What is already known

  • Antipsychotics are used to treat conditions like schizophrenia and bipolar disorder. They are not related to euthanasia drugs, and the doses used in treatment are not close to a lethal dose in the way I once suggested.
  • I expect a similar overlap in ingredient lists between two unrelated tablet types, because the same fillers are so widely used. I have not yet checked this, and checking it is the test below.

How the theory could be tested

The test would be to repeat the comparison using the active ingredients only, and to compare it with a control, for example the overlap between antipsychotics and an unrelated drug such as ibuprofen. If the "euthanasia" overlap is no higher than the control, the idea fails.

Safety

Please do not stop or change any prescribed medicine because of this page. Stopping an antipsychotic suddenly can be dangerous. Talk to your prescriber or pharmacist first. If you are in crisis, call or text 988 in the US.
